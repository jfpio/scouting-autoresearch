## Translated text

The patrol observes for several minutes a section of terrain with boundaries precisely defined by the patrol leader (a few hundred meters in width and depth). After the observation, they move to a concealed spot from which this section is not visible.

Meanwhile, several scouts alter the appearance of the terrain: either removing certain objects, rearranging them, or adding things that were not there before. For example, they might take one log from a woodpile and place it aside on the ground; turn a cart by its shaft in the opposite direction; move a large stone and chalk another; remove previously stuck branches that imitated a bush and place them elsewhere; and finally, they themselves hide behind the terrain’s cover. (All changes made must be recorded).

At a signal given by them, the patrol emerges from hiding and tries to catch the changes that, as if by a magician’s wand, have occurred in the terrain. Each scout observes separately and notes the changes they notice. After the observation is complete, at a given signal, the perpetrators of this change in the scene emerge from hiding.

Beforehand, the patrol leader instructs that observation must not be haphazard: first, the closer terrain is observed, then the farther, moving in sections across and in depth.

This game can be turned into a competition between two patrols.
