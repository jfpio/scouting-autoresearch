## Translated text

Individually, in pairs, or in entire patrols, scouts set out to meet "enemies" distant by some 3 kilometers. To prevent them from straying off course, they may be positioned on a main road or given a direction in open terrain according to some distant visible landmark toward which they must head. Such a landmark could be a steep hill, a tall tree, or a church tower lying in the enemy's rear. The patrol that first spots the opponent is considered the winner. The patrol leader then raises the flag high and gives a whistle signal to notify the entire patrol of the discovery. It is not necessary for patrols to march in a tight group; however, since the patrol whose leader first raises the flag wins, scouts should remain in constant contact through agreed-upon signals, calls, and messengers. All ruses are permitted, such as climbing trees, hiding in wagons, and the like—only disguising oneself is forbidden. Such a game can also be organized at night.
