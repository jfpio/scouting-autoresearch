## Translated text

Observation can be directed not only at specially indicated objects but also at everything encountered during the march—both the terrain configuration to the right and left of the road and objects near and far. Moreover, observations should be memorized. This forms a distinct exercise:

It is announced that close observation of the road over a distance of, for example, 3 km is required. After covering this distance—a halt is called, and the leader asks a series of questions: *"What were the horses like at the first cart passed? — Who was sitting on it? — What was interesting on the roof of the small church? — What inscription was on the roadside chapel? — How many telegraph wires ran along the road? — What was heard at the entrance to the bridge? — What was on the left bank of the river right by the bridge?"* and so on.

As before, each person writes down their answers without revealing them to the others, and then the answers are scored in favor of the scout or their patrol. The patrol leader must, of course, note all observations during the march that they will later ask about.

The exercise is significantly easier if it is announced that questions will only concern nature, or only artificial objects passed during the march, or actions seen along the way, or unusual things, etc. It will also be easier if questions are asked immediately after passing a given object rather than only after completing the entire route.
