## Translated text

We are a military patrol tasked with investigating the position of village A, located on the opposite edge of the forest. To achieve this, we must determine the directional angle using a Bézard compass based on the map and use it to reach the given village. Individual groups—patrols—are dispatched every 5 minutes. After all patrols have been sent out, the instructor travels by the nearest route (a bicycle is useful) to village A, where they receive the arriving patrols. The patrol that reaches the destination in the shortest time (patrols may calculate the march time based on the map—this will help with orientation) and with the smallest deviation from the march direction wins. Each group should have a compass. The march should be conducted without a map.

More challenging objectives in such a march may include smaller points such as: road intersections or bends, a forester’s lodge, a hill, etc.

To save time during the exercise, we may shorten the directional march route. It is sufficient for the marching group to reach a line (a road, forest clearing, etc.) that intersects the march direction. The exercise may end at such a line. Patrols mark their arrival point and step aside to avoid interfering with others' independence. The instructor assesses which point was most consistent with the designated directional angle. However, such forms should be avoided as they are artificial and weaken the value of the game. It is better to bring the march objectives closer by taking a cross-country route.

Initially, the march through the forest will be short, around 1—2 kilometers, while in later exercises for more experienced groups, it may extend up to 10 kilometers. Such exercises can be excellently conducted on larger, branching lakes where the destinations are not visible.

More difficult exercises will involve games over a larger area, such as individual cross-country marches in which youth will use a compass and map to determine direction.

Another type will include exercises and games based on sketches or maps.
