## Translated text

Kim's Game is one of the standard scouting exercises; frequent practice develops observation skills and visual memory. It was introduced by the English writer Rudyard Kipling in his novel *Kim*.

Each patrol member prepares a collection of small objects: various penknives, fabric samples, pencils, pens, stones, buttons, and the like, which they keep in a small bag.

For beginners (in the first few games), only **15** objects are arranged; for those more practiced, **20** to **30**. After placing the objects not too close together on a table, they are covered with a cloth. Scouts sit around the table so that everyone has a good view; the cloth is lifted for **1** minute, after which the objects are covered again. Instead of watching a clock, one may count aloud at a moderate pace up to **60**.

Then, within a few minutes (for beginners **4**, on average **3** minutes), scouts make a list of the objects they remember from those they saw. After several or a dozen exercises, difficulties should be introduced by requiring not only listing the objects but also describing, drawing them, and so on. Each time, a different scout arranges the objects; the patrol leader records the game's results by noting next to the scout's name (or number) and the date a fraction where the numerator indicates the number of objects remembered and the denominator the number of objects viewed, e.g., No. **3** **26/VII** **17/24**. By comparing these records over a longer period, you will see the progress made in training visual memory.
