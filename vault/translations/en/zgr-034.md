A favorite game among boys, especially those in urban areas, suitable for higher grades of primary schools and lower grades of secondary schools. A well-defined section of a forest serves as the playing field; a particular corner of the forest is designated as the "castle," where haiduks bring captured bandits. Participants, numbering **12—50**, divide into two camps: the haiduks led by the elder and the bandits led by the hetman. The haiduks wear a visible badge (e.g., a scarf on the shoulder). At the start of the game, the haiduks do not leave the castle for a prearranged time agreed upon by the elder and the hetman (no more than **10** minutes), giving the bandits an opportunity to hide. After this time elapses, the elder sends the haiduks out in several detachments in different directions. A bandit struck three times in succession by a hand is taken prisoner and must go to the castle, where they remain under guard. A prisoner can only be freed by a free bandit who sneaks into the castle undetected and strikes them three times; this succeeds best when several bandits storm in simultaneously to liberate their comrades. The game lasts for a prearranged amount of time (e.g., one hour). Halfway through, the roles of the two camps are switched. The camp that has taken more prisoners wins.
