The art of drawing conclusions from reading tracks is just as important as the skill of tracking itself. Tracks are merely the material from which one builds, i.e., derives the appropriate conclusions.

One scout walks across terrain where visible tracks remain. At intervals, he changes his gait

![img-0.jpeg](https://jfpio.github.io/harcerz-w-polu/book/assets/page-123-img-0.jpeg)

Fig. 30.

or performs certain actions strictly according to the patrol leader’s instructions. For example, in one place he makes a large leap, in another he limps heavily on his right leg, then he sits on the ground, removes one shoe, and continues walking barefoot on one foot; elsewhere, he crawls on his knees, etc. After a quarter of an hour, the patrol sets off along the tracks and must deduce, from the various changes in the trail, what the tracked scout did at each location.

Such tracks work particularly well in snow.
